# Why this exists

Reader: newcomer.

## A birthday recap I never finished

End of December 2025. My son's birthday was coming up and I wanted a recap of his year. I had
hundreds of clips, all safely in Immich.

I opened Premiere, imported 40 clips, dragged them onto a timeline, added a few crossfades,
realised I needed music. Two hours later I had 90 seconds of mediocre output and 360 clips I
hadn't looked at. Never finished it.

Storage wasn't the problem (Immich handles that), and neither was finding clips (search works).
The problem is the 4 to 8 hours between "I have 400 clips" and "here's a 3-minute film I'd show
my family".

## What it does instead

It does the boring part. It connects to Immich read-only, looks at the period I pick and builds
the film the way I would: find the stories in the period (a birthday, a weekend away, the
ordinary weeks at home), give the big ones more room, keep the favourites, prefer a video when a
video carries the moment, and keep everything in the order it happened. Then it renders it with
titles, a map for a trip, and music.

All of that works on a plain NAS. A GPU makes it faster, and a model makes the cut better: it
reads the draft and swaps out the shots that add nothing. Worth having if you can, not required.

I still review what it picked. But I'm reviewing 30 pictures in a storyboard instead of scrolling
through 400, and when it leaves one out, `runs why` says which rule did it. Every feature traces
back to one manual step I didn't want to do again: that's why Live Photos get stitched, why a trip
opens on a map, and why the music ducks under the clips' own sound.

If you run Immich, you already decided to own your pictures. This gives you something to do with
them.

## Built with AI, on purpose

Claude writes the code; I make the decisions, test the films and debug what breaks. The
experiment is how far AI can go on a codebase this size while the code stays clean.

So far, guardrails are what make it work. Every PR goes through the gates in the `Makefile` (lint, types,
complexity, an 800-line file cap, dead code, duplication, architecture boundaries, security scans,
docs drift) and the build stays red until they pass. They catch real bugs that Claude introduces
with a straight face. The file cap forced a composition-based design with zero mixins, which
turned out to be the right call anyway.

The judgement calls stay human: what a good film is, which rule is right, when a cut is good
enough to ship. [DISCLAIMER.md](https://github.com/sam-dumont/immich-video-memory-generator/blob/main/DISCLAIMER.md)
lists the gates one by one and where the approach fell short.

## What it isn't

- **Not a video editor.** You tick and untick pictures and cut again. There is no timeline.
- **Not a photo manager.** It needs Immich and only reads from it, unless you ask it to upload
  the film back.
- **Not instant.** The first cut over a period reads the pictures it can use; that work is
  banked, so the next cut over the same period is mostly the render.
- **No mobile app.** A web UI that works in a phone browser, and a CLI.
