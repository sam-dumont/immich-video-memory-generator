# Supplement: how working editors mark selects (sourced quotes)

Raw material from a supplementary research pass on editors' selects/rating
practice. All quotes carry their source; the researcher's caveat: quotes were
fetched via an extractor without live web search — verify against the source
page before quoting in public. Feeds the main report
(`editing-craft-research.md`) — kept separate so provenance stays clear.

## Binary or graded? Both exist, named.

GRADED:
- David Tillman (documentary editor), Art of the Cut, ProVideoCoalition 2018
  (provideocoalition.com/art-cut-documentary-editor-david-tillman/):
  assistants use Favorites in FCPX, "then go a step further… denote any
  'can't miss' type material with asterisks. Then I'm able to easily search
  for asterisks." A literal selects vs must-use two-tier system.
- T. Payton, Frame.io Insider 2016 (blog.frame.io/2016/10/18/make-fcp-x-log-
  better-1/): "I needed more degrees of Favorites. I needed a 'Great!' as
  well as 'Just Okay'" — built 1-5 star keywords.
- Chad Beck (The Last Dance), PVC 2020 (provideocoalition.com/aotc-the-last-
  dance/): graded by TRACK HEIGHT — good bites "I'd bump it up to track two…
  if there's something REALLY good… track three, and that way I have a
  visual." Three tiers, zero rating UI.

BINARY:
- Ben Mercer (Unknown Soldier), PVC 2018: "favorites – which are green lines
  – and then the red lines, which are rejected moments"; "Selection —
  highlighting and rejecting — is central to editing"; "A selects reel is
  basically like 'favorites'."
- Pippa Ehrlich (My Octopus Teacher), PVC 2021 (attribution caveat:
  co-editor Dan Schwalm; page lacked per-quote tags): "you can tell when
  you're watching something that's really compelling, so, that stuff just
  goes in."

## Against elaborate schemes (the load-bearing section)

- Gabriel Rhodes (Time), PVC 2020 (provideocoalition.com/aotc-time/): "I
  don't categorize anything. I don't try and put it into baskets. I just
  want to really respond to the material… Something about putting stuff into
  categories or into baskets is just going to limit my interaction with it."
  His replacement: volume of flat annotation ("fifty-five markers" in one
  clip) plus REWATCHING — "once the edit started to unfold, it would
  re-contextualize those moments" — a direct argument that EARLY HARD
  REJECTS ARE WRONG.
- T. Payton, post-mortem of his own scheme: "a majority of those keywords
  went unused as they were not applicable to the story… Marking everything
  with spur of the moment keywords was not the answer" — defer taxonomy
  until story structure exists.
- Jabez Olssen (Get Back), Frame.io 2021: granularity drifts upward — "after
  a while, you're not really selecting just individual shots, you're
  selecting scenes."
- Todd Douglas Miller (Apollo 11): "I just have a bin that says footage…
  I just saturated myself with the footage."
- Counterweight (Hullfish quote essay, PVC 2018): Tom Cross — "The goal is
  to organize the material and understand it. A lot of times that's about
  figuring out what can be ignored"; Tom McArdle — "A lot of editing is
  simply a process of organizing and narrowing down your options." Nobody
  says organizing is worthless; the resistance targets TAXONOMY/TIERS,
  and comes from documentary editors facing unstructured material.
- UNVERIFIED (could not locate source): Pax Wassermann, "Selects become a
  burden at a certain point" (attributed to a PremiumBeat write-up of a 2015
  Sight Sound & Story panel). Treat as unconfirmed.

## Mechanics worth knowing

- Andy Grieve (Going Clear): subtractive selects — "I'll literally just cut
  out the questions and the dead air and end up with a sequence that's just
  the best parts"; master selects reel per interview, locators as index.
- Inbal B. Lessner (Seduced): PARALLEL SELECTS BANKS per material type —
  "I do interview selects and I do verite selects and I do archival selects
  and that's my own little bank"; delegated conventions ("put a smiley face
  … I will just look for a smiley face").
- Devin Concannon (The Last Dance): topic-scoped selects on demand — "I need
  everybody that's gonna talk about this one thing… gather a selects reel
  that is specific to that topic."
- Sofi Marshall (Frame.io 2018): labeling/favoriting is "more than half the
  work of cutting together an assembly with your selects."
- Felicia Livingston (features): selects reels ONLY WHERE THEY PAY ("action
  sequences… a lot of crying and a lot of takes").

## What this says for the pipeline (2 lines)

Marks are cheap and flat early (binary keep/reject, or flat markers), tiers
appear only for "can't miss" (the favourites law is that tier), taxonomy
before story structure is the documented failure mode — and hard early
rejects are the thing multiple working editors explicitly refuse.
