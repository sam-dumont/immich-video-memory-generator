# Your First Memory

One month, start to finish. A month is the right first cut: one minute of video, and the slow
part (reading every picture once) is bounded by what one month holds.

## Before you start

- **Immich v2 or v3** and an API key (Account Settings > API Keys; grant **All**, or at least read
  access to assets, people, albums, timeline and search, plus upload and album rights if you want
  the video back in Immich).
- **Docker**, or **Python 3.11 or later**.
- About **22 GB of disk** for the caches at their default budgets (10 GB of downloaded video kept
  7 days, 10 GB of previews, 2 GB of clip previews).
- One of the three ways to run: rules only needs nothing else; the classifiers need
  `immich-memories models fetch`; the model reader needs a caption server and a reader. The
  [self-hosting guide](../deploy/self-hosting.md) stands them up in order.

## Launch

Docker:

```bash
cat > .env << 'EOF'
IMMICH_URL=https://photos.example.com
IMMICH_API_KEY=your-api-key-here
EOF
curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sam-dumont/immich-video-memory-generator/main/docker-compose.yml
docker compose up -d
```

Or without Docker:

```bash
uvx --from "immich-memories[editorial]" immich-memories ui
```

Open [http://localhost:8080](http://localhost:8080). Both routes keep the UI on localhost. If the
app and your browser are on different machines, read the
[Docker page](../deploy/installation/docker.md#quick-start) first: it pairs publishing the port
with turning on authentication.

## Connect and pick a month

With credentials in the config or the environment the page connects on its own and shows the
brief. Otherwise fill the **Immich Connection** panel and press **Test Connection**.

Choose **Monthly Highlights** and a month with a fair number of pictures. Leave **Auto duration**
on.

## Cut

Press **Cut**. Five phase rows follow the run, the counted stages draw a bar with an estimate,
and a model server that stops answering is named on the Editing row within a second. Reloading
mid-cut lands on the same rows. The full anatomy of this screen is on the
[Memory page](./web-ui/memory.mdx#cut).

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-cutting" alt="The cut in progress" />

## Read the storyboard

When it finishes, the page opens on the **Storyboard**: the cut in the order it plays, one shot
per picture with its timecode, day, the story it belongs to and the editor's one-line reason.
The **Story** tab shows the same cut the way the editor weighed it, heaviest story first.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-story" alt="The storyboard of the first cut" />

If a picture should not be there, **Review the pool** opens the media pool with the cut's picks:
untick it, or tick one the editor dropped, and press **Cut again**. Your ticks hold.

## Export and watch

Press **Export**, then **Generate Video**. The status line walks through downloading the clips,
assembling with transitions and titles, music and delivery. Music, title and resolution sit one
click away under **Back to Generation Options**; the defaults are fine for a first run.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-export" alt="Export: output filename and Generate Video" />

The player shows the result and **Saved to** gives the path (a per-run folder under
`~/Videos/Memories` by default). With **Upload after generation** on, it is also in the Immich
album you named.

## What just happened

The app fetched the month's metadata and previews, prepared facts for every eligible picture,
read the stories those pictures show, gave each story the pictures its weight earned, and
assembled them in chronological order with FFmpeg, titles and music. Everything it read is
banked, so the second cut over the same month is mostly the render. Every attempt is kept under
the cache directory with the plan and the reason for every picture, and `immich-memories runs
story` prints the same storyboard in the terminal.
