Immich Memories reads a period of your self-hosted [Immich](https://immich.app) library (a
month, a year, a trip, one person across the years), picks the pictures and videos that tell it,
keeps them in the order they were taken, and renders the film with titles, maps and music.

## Works on a plain NAS, better with a GPU or a model

The default install is one container on the box that already runs Immich. It cuts the film from
what your library already knows: dates, places, favourites, the people Immich recognised, and
what a few small pinned classifiers say about each picture (a screen, a document, sensitive
content, who is in frame). Those run on the CPU and are fetched once with `models fetch`.
Nothing else to host, no API key, no account.

That already makes a film worth sharing. It needs two things from you: where home is, so a week
away counts as a trip, and who is who, so close family gets its place in the film.
[Teach it your family](../get-started/who-is-who.md) covers both.

Start with the [Quick start](../get-started/quick-start.md), then cut one month with
[Your first film](../get-started/first-film.mdx). [Requirements](../run/requirements.md) says
which box is enough.

## What you get

- Ten memory types: a year in review, a season, a month, one person, several people
  (`"Riley" AND ("Casey" OR "Bob")`), on this day, an album, a trip with its map, a holiday
  across years, and a day the library itself flagged. The web UI adds a custom date range.
- Photos, videos and Live Photos in one cut, HDR kept end to end.
- Title screens, month dividers, and for a trip an animated map from home to where you went.
- Music: your own file or one of 28 bundled tracks, ducked under the clips' own sound.
- A storyboard before anything renders: every shot in playing order, with its day and its
  story. Untick what you disagree with and cut again; `immich-memories runs why <asset-id>`
  says which step kept a picture or left it out.

## What a GPU or a model adds

Each of these makes the film better or faster, and all of them are optional. They plug into the
same install, and what they add is banked next to what the NAS already worked out, so switching
one off later costs nothing.

| Add | What it buys | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|
| A GPU | the same film, sooner | encoding on the NAS's own iGPU ([Hardware encoding](../run/hardware.md)), preparation on a [GPU box](../better/inference.md) |
| A reader model | it reads the finished draft, names the shots that add nothing and fills their seats with better ones. It does not re-plan the film | a [local or hosted model](../better/reader.md) |
| Captions | a line of description under every picture, for the reader and the family-viewing check | a [caption server](../better/captions.md) |
| Generated music | an original track for each film (ACE-Step or MusicGen) | a [music server](../better/music.md) |

What each one costs in hardware, time and euros is on
[What a model adds, what it costs](../better/overview.md).

## What leaves your network

A default run talks to your Immich server and nothing else. No telemetry, no cloud. Immich stays
read-only: the film goes back into it only when you ask for the upload. Every outside host sits
behind a switch that is off in a fresh install, and [Privacy](../run/privacy.md) lists them.

## Why it is being reworked so much

Some parts are done and stay put: the install (one compose file, one image), how it uses Immich
(read-only unless you upload), and the render (titles, maps, music, HDR, encoding).

The part that keeps moving is selection: which pictures make the cut. It changes most weeks, and
every change is checked against films cut from real libraries before it merges. So a new release
can pick a slightly different set for the same month than the last one did, and a config key that
goes away is warned about and dropped, never a crash. To keep selection from shifting under you,
pin a version tag of the image instead of `latest`. Either way, watch a film before you send it to the family.
