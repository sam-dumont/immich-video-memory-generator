# Your first film

Reader: newcomer.

One month, start to finish, in the web UI, then the same thing on the CLI. A month is the right
first cut: about a minute of film, and the slow part (reading each picture once) is bounded by
what one month holds.

This page assumes the [Quick start](./quick-start.md) is done: the container is up and
`models fetch` has run. Without the models a cut refuses to start and says
`Run immich-memories models fetch`. On pip, it's the same commands without the
`docker compose exec immich-memories` prefix: [uv or pip](../run/uv-pip.md).

## Pick a month

Open [http://localhost:8080](http://localhost:8080). With `IMMICH_URL` and `IMMICH_API_KEY` set,
the page connects on its own and shows the brief. If it doesn't, open **Advanced**, fill the
**Immich Connection** panel and press **Test Connection**.

Choose **Monthly Highlights** and a month with a fair number of pictures and a few videos. Leave
**Auto duration** on.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-brief" alt="The brief, connected to Immich" />

## Cut

Press **Cut**. One row per phase follows the run: reading dates, places and people, then the
pictures, then the edit. The counted stages draw a bar with an estimate, and reloading the page
mid-cut lands on the same rows.

If `immich-memories models fetch` has not run on this host, the cut does not start and nothing is
asked of Immich. The brief comes back with a red card that names the missing model and the
command to run, and it stays there until the next **Cut**.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-cutting" alt="The cut in progress" />

The first cut reads every picture the film can reach: the month's pictures, their Live Photo clips
and the bursts around them. Each one goes through the eight context heads and the two detectors
once, and the answers are banked. The second cut of the same month reads nothing again.

## Read the storyboard

When the cut finishes, the page opens on the **Storyboard**: the film in the order it plays, one
shot per row, with its timecode, its day, the story it belongs to and the reason the editor kept
it. The **Story** tab shows the same cut the way the editor weighed it, heaviest story first.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-story" alt="The storyboard of the first cut" />

A few things to look for on a first cut:

- **It's chronological.** Always. A film never jumps back in time.
- **Favourites come first.** A picture you starred in Immich wins its moment over the others.
- **Videos lead.** When a video carries a moment, it beats a still of the same moment.
- **Quiet weeks can be empty.** A week at home with no favourite, no video, no close family and no
  unusually busy day gets no shot. The film goes short rather than fill it with a guess.

Why each rule exists, and every number behind it, is on
[How it chooses](../how-it-chooses/overview.md).

## Fix it with two clicks

**Review the pool** opens the media pool with the cut's picks ticked. Untick a picture the editor
kept and it's out; tick one it dropped and it's in, in its own story at its own time. Press
**Cut again**. Your ticks hold until **Start over**. The one thing that outranks a tick is the
family-viewing gate.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-pool-outcomes" alt="The media pool after a cut, with the picks ticked" />

Two minutes in the pool before the first cut pays off too: untick the pocket recording and the
45-second clip of a wall. Twins and bursts the editor judges itself; what it can't know is what
you would never show anyone.

More ways to steer it (stars, people, `runs why`) are on
[Overrule it](../how-it-chooses/overrule-it.md).

## Export and watch

Press **Export**, then **Generate Video**. The status line walks through downloading the clips,
assembling with titles and transitions, the music, and delivery. Music, title and resolution sit
one click away under **Back to Generation Options**; the defaults are fine for a first film.

<ThemedScreenshot name="memory-export" alt="Export: output filename and Generate Video" />

The player shows the result. **Saved to** gives the path: `./output` beside your compose file on
Docker, `~/Videos/Memories` on pip, one folder per run. **Length** is measured from the finished
file. With **Upload after generation** on, the film also goes to the Immich album you named,
tagged as this app's own so a later run never films it again.

## The same on the CLI

```bash
alias im='docker compose exec immich-memories immich-memories'
im generate --memory-type monthly_highlights --year 2025 --month 6
im runs story        # the storyboard of the last run, in the terminal
im runs why <asset-id>   # what the run decided about one picture, and why
```

`generate` prepares, cuts and renders in one go. To read a bigger window ahead of time (a whole
year, overnight on a NAS), run `im prepare --year 2025`: it reads and banks, and cuts nothing.
Every cut inside that year then starts warm. All flags: [generate](../make/cli/generate.md),
[prepare](../make/cli/prepare.md).
